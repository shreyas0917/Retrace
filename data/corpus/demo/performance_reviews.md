# Performance Review Process

## Cycle Overview

The company runs one formal performance review each year, completed in March, plus a lighter mid-year check-in in September. Reviews cover the calendar year. The process is managed in the performance tool, and HR publishes the exact dates each January.

## Annual Review Timeline

- January 31: promotion nominations are due from managers.
- February 15: employee self-assessments are due.
- March 1: manager assessments are due.
- March 1 to March 15: calibration sessions are held within each department.
- March 31: reviews are shared with employees in one-on-one meetings.
- April 1: merit increases and promotions take effect.

Employees who joined after October 1 receive a short informal review instead of a full rating.

## Rating Scale

Performance is rated on a 5-point scale:

1. Does Not Meet Expectations
2. Partially Meets Expectations
3. Meets Expectations
4. Exceeds Expectations
5. Greatly Exceeds Expectations

A rating of 3 describes solid, fully satisfactory performance and is the most common rating. Ratings are calibrated across teams so that similar contributions receive similar ratings; there is no forced distribution.

## Goals

Goals are set at the start of the year in the OKR tool. Each employee should have no more than 5 objectives, each with measurable key results. Goals are revisited at the September mid-year check-in and may be changed with manager agreement. Goal attainment informs but does not mechanically determine the rating.

## Peer Feedback

During the review, each employee selects 3 to 5 peers to give 360-degree feedback, and the manager may add reviewers. Peer feedback is submitted by February 22 and is shared with the employee in summarized form. Feedback focuses on behaviors and impact, not personality.

## Compensation Outcomes

Merit increases are funded from a department budget and differentiated by rating. Compensation bands are reviewed each year against market data, and employees are told their band placement during the review conversation. Promotions require a written case from the manager, approval in calibration, and an open position at the next level.

## Performance Improvement Plans

An employee rated 1 or 2 may be placed on a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP). A PIP lasts a minimum of 60 days and includes specific goals, weekly check-ins, and a written outcome at the end. HR must review every PIP before it is shared with the employee.

## Mid-Year Check-In

The September check-in is a documented conversation about progress against goals, development, and workload. It produces no rating and no compensation change, but notes from it are used as input to the March review.

## Appeals

Employees who believe a rating is unfair may appeal in writing to HR within 10 business days of receiving it. HR reviews the evidence with the manager's manager and responds within 15 business days.
